# RFC 0002: Guide a blocked mission from diagnosis to verified recovery

**Status:** Draft for discussion. **Baseline:** Kepler 3.2.0. **Companion:** [RFC 0001: Mission progression testing](0001-mission-progression-testing.md).

## Decision requested

Let an agent ask Kepler three questions about the original mission: *What prevents progress? Which repairs preserve which work? What evidence or decision makes the next step safe?* Add read-only diagnosis and plan generation, plus one narrow operation to retain approved evidence. Use the existing migration, reset, reconstruction, and publication engines to perform repairs. Do not give an agent a general-purpose repair command or make a new mission the default answer.

## Incident and existing contract

The supplied November 24 session (`01a20e6b-22d3-7470-93e8-f34965b368af`, JSONL records 29, 41, 59, and 69–78) documents two different blockers:

- **History:** The agent verified the 3.2.0 executable and matching resources. `recovery inspect` found a legacy read-only layout; the inventory contained 26 sources and reported no pending operations, archives, or raw inspection issues. Yet `workflow construction unit show` reported a blocked unit and `unordered-transitions`. Across 17 Construction journals and 22 readable events, the causes did not order the Quality Requirements start before its revision end and reopen. The agent correctly ruled out format migration. The [3.2.0 writer fix](../releases/3.2.0.md) prevents new missing predecessor links; it does not supply them to old history.
- **Authority to repair:** Two other Pi sessions were present. An idle pane and an empty workflow-run listing did not establish terminal clients, reconciled effects, or the absence of external writers. The requester chose a **same-mission clean restart** and said they would stop the clients. That was a planning choice, not confirmation or approval of a manifest. The session ended before evidence-file writes, reset preview, or apply. No manifest hash was available; no mission or worktree files changed. The record does not show a failing apply, a lost archive, or whether recovery would have succeeded after those prerequisites were met.

Today [`inspect` and `status`](../cli/history-recovery.md) inventory raw material without requiring a healthy projection. But `migrate`, `reset`, and `publish` preview a **caller-supplied complete manifest**. `reconstruct` with `phase: "prepare"` consumes a supplied plan and writes a private draft; `phase: "check"` rereads it. The lead must also supply strictly bound [client](../recovery-client-compatibility.md) and [run/effect](../recovery-lead-evidence.md) evidence. In the session, the agent consulted implementation and test fixtures to work out what had to exist *before* it could obtain a preview hash. This RFC changes that handoff. The [accepted recovery contract and its amendments](../specs/v3/mission-history-reset-recovery.md) remain the authority for history changes.

## Diagnose without writing

**Proposed `kepler recovery diagnose`** takes the same mission and project selectors as `inspect`. Start from the raw snapshot, even with a damaged selector or an unreadable phase file. Report unreadable bytes as such. Probe the normal projection separately for each identifiable unit; a failed probe is a finding, not a reason to discard the inventory. Correlate source IDs, journal-local positions, event IDs, `caused_by` edges, current control, pending intents, and retained material. Never order concurrent events by time, directory order, or attempt ID. If inputs change during inspection, mark the diagnosis stale and offer a fresh read.

Return a versioned report on stdout: mission/unit binding, binary and resource identity, observed selection or legacy boundary, inventory digest, findings, candidate routes, and next requests. Each finding names the violated rule, affected scope, supporting references (including byte ranges where relevant), facts still unknown, and who can supply them. Separate what Kepler read from what a host or requester attested. The report takes no mutation claim and writes no case file. A new session can recompute it; a saved copy helps discussion but grants no authority.

| Finding | Offered route | Required limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valid selected history; interrupted ordinary work | Return to [`kepler-resume`](../recovery.md) and the owning operation. | Reuse the exact attempt after termination/effect checks; do not reset healthy history. |
| Direct-layout history with validated, unchanged workflow meaning | Use `migrate` with the matched released guide and exact source retirement. | `migration_required` alone never makes the route eligible. |
| Unordered transitions or other semantic damage with readable originals | Compare reconstruction of a provable subset against a same-mission clean restart. | Never repair meaning with a format conversion. Show what each choice would preserve, exclude, and require doing again. |
| Exact retained intent with partial effects | Inspect `status`, original instructions, archives, and observed effects; propose the exact retry if they match. | Do not allocate a competing operation or replay a completed one over later work. |
| Conflicting evidence, unsafe/unreadable material, unknown writer, or unsupported damage | Explain the blocked step, the specific evidence needed, and the responsible owner. If no supported rule applies, return `unsupported` with a local diagnostic summary for a maintainer. | Do not relabel unreadable files as missing or guess an edit. New proof or a reviewed rule can reopen planning. |

These findings can coexist. Report the layout and the ordering defect together; the latter rules out migration. Unresolved ownership blocks applying **any** otherwise viable repair. An unfinished operation takes precedence over a new one. If two viable repairs change different facts or discard different progress, show the difference and let the requester choose. Do not select one by a confidence score.

## Propose a repair before it is eligible

**Proposed `kepler recovery plan`** reads a strict request on stdin with the selected findings, repair preference, target continuation, and any evidence references already available. It rereads the bound snapshot. Each option reports retained identities and progress, affected source ranges, proposed writes or retirements, known losses, approvals/checks to obtain again, and the first permitted continuation. If a reconstruction depends on more evidence, mark that result provisional. Show the last provable point rather than promising a specific stage. A defect need not have a name if the general reconstruction verifier can prove a safe subset and continuation. Otherwise escalate it without proposing journal edits.

The result distinguishes `needs-evidence`, `needs-choice`, `ready-for-preview`, and `unsupported`. A missing fact has an owner, an acceptable observation, and the operation to rerun. A blocked plan may have an inventory digest; it has **no executable manifest hash**. Once migration, reset, or publication has its prerequisites, the planner builds the complete typed manifest and invokes that operation's existing read-only preview. Only its verifier may return a canonical `manifest_hash` for approval. Freeze proposed IDs and input references across reruns of that request. A later reset receipt or checked candidate supplies the inputs for the *next* operation; no advance approval of an entire route.

**Evidence before preview.** The lead gathers host records or requester statements through authorized means. Kepler can derive byte lengths, hashes, and bindings; it cannot decide a process stopped or that an approval occurred. The planner proposes the exact evidence-file writes on stdout, with full reviewable bytes, purpose, destination under the mission's recovery evidence area, source references, and expected prior state. It can calculate the lead report against the proposed inventory using the [existing self-exclusion rules](../recovery-lead-evidence.md); a simulated inventory cannot authorize reset.

**Proposed `kepler recovery evidence`** accepts a preview/apply envelope solely for retaining those records. Its approval binds the mission, baseline, exact files, bytes, requester, and decision. Typed evidence roles determine the allowed paths; no user-supplied shell or writes to draft, archive, or intent storage. Its digest cannot serve as a migration, reset, or publication hash. Under shared coordination, create approved absent files; accept identical prior writes on exact retry, reject unexpected bytes and unsafe paths, and report partial writes by path. It must work without a healthy old projection and must never write journals, a selector, project code, or host configuration. After retention, rerun inspection and the real preview against the on-disk bytes. Do not retain credentials or unrelated host records.

Evidence writes do not endorse their contents. A stop request, an idle status, or a generated statement proves no termination. Changed evidence requires a new decision. Use a new path for new source material. If an unconsumed generated lead report must change at its fixed path, propose a separate replacement: bind and preserve its old bytes at a reviewed path, verify them before replacement, and invalidate the old preview or draft. Never replace evidence still required by an active selection or pending apply. Conflicting or user-authored files remain untouched. Rebuild and check any affected draft before asking for publication.

### Constructing a retained-progress option

Use the [existing reconstruction actions](../../packages/cli/src/history/reconstruction.ts). Start with verified source ranges. Retain exact events only if their causes, references, required artifacts, approvals, and checkpoint evidence close under replay. If a transition is ambiguous, identify dependent attempts, Activities, and decisions that also lose support. Offer the earlier safe continuation and list the work it would cost. An independently supported correction can `replace` or `add` a fact with a fresh event identity, explicit authority, and a validated mapping for its dependents. `exclude` leaves the original in the archive. Account for every required source range; do not reissue an old approval or claim a check passed because a file exists.

A clean restart instead proposes no accepted prior progress. Reconfirm the original mission, known units and workspace checkpoints, preserve the exact original request text, and keep code and documents in place. If the original text is missing or conflicting, require the [specific requester assertion](../recovery-reconstruction-evidence.md#exact-original-request), not a generic restart decision. Neither option edits the originals. A user may decline both proposals and leave the mission unchanged.

## Worked example: the Quality revision

At the recorded stopping point the agent could have given the following answer from the proposed diagnosis and plan. This is an illustration, not a new observation or a usable manifest:

> I found the Quality start and revision records, but no causal order between the start and its ending. I cannot migrate this history unchanged into a usable unit. You chose a clean restart of this mission and unit. Two other clients have not been confirmed stopped. Please confirm their termination and identify any external clients or offline copies. I will reconcile their runs, file effects, and locks, then show the exact evidence files I propose to retain. Nothing has been written; there is no reset hash to approve yet.

If the missing proof arrives, the proposed sequence is:

1. Verify actual `mission-history-generations/v1` support for the selected client and matched resources; inventory affected runs and known copies. Keep incompatible or unverified clients stopped. Reconcile effects even if the final affected-run list is empty. With the requester's approval, retain the precise evidence bytes and rerun the reset preview. If any client remains unknown and potentially active, stop here.
2. Show the complete reset manifest and canonical hash, verified input inventory, proposed private archive, the exact 17 direct journal paths to retire **if still unchanged**, and the empty `recovering` state. Obtain exact reset approval. Immediately before apply, the lead rechecks actual client state; the executor rechecks sources, evidence, and ownership under its fence. It preserves and reads back the available original bytes under the Git common directory before retirement. Ordinary workflow mutations remain blocked after reset.
3. Obtain the distinct clean-restart authority. Gather the publication-specific compatibility and lead reports against the selected reset. With evidence-write approval, retain them **before** preparing the candidate: its `required_material.evidence` binds their exact hashes. Present the plan, candidate ID, and private draft effects for approval, then use `reconstruct` with `phase: "prepare"` and `phase: "check"`. The draft keeps the original mission, unit, request, and checkpoints but restores no prior completion or approval. Preview the checked manifest through `publish`; obtain separate approval for this publication and recheck actual clients before applying it.
4. Read the selected generation through the normal gateway. Verify archive contents, approved source disposition, unchanged unrelated dirty files, unit membership, expected projection, and the new `history_version`. Report the receipt, remaining work and approvals, and the checked continuation. Here the next action is **read-only inspection**. Do not dispatch Functional Design. Later work must carry the published version and its own authority; stale copies must complete a verified join first.

Only the stop above was observed. These four steps are the proposed route out, not a claim that they ran. Static evidence cannot detect an undeclared client restarting outside Kepler. The host or lead owns that operational check; a provider-specific process scanner in the CLI would not remove the limit.

## Interruption, extension, and rollout

On interruption, read `status` and reconcile the actual effects before retrying. Keep the original approved envelopes and identities. If bytes, guide, ownership, evidence, or requested effects changed, stop and replan; do not refresh a stale approval. If a previous apply completed, return its historical receipt separately from the current selection. If the new selection is unsuitable, a further separately approved recovery can use preserved bytes. Never roll back by copying an old selector, truncating a journal, removing a lock, or resetting the Git worktree.

Ship the first diagnostic rules in the CLI alongside the owning projection and history contracts. Each rule declares a stable ID/version, applicable formats, evidence it reads, the defect it can distinguish, supported operation types, and a postcondition. The planner may compose those operations; only their existing executors change history. To add a new defect, contribute a rule and a positive/negative [regression case](0001-mission-progression-testing.md). A repair requiring a new kind of write needs a reviewed executor and its own approval contract. A guide, model suggestion, or externally supplied script cannot introduce effects at runtime.

Roll out in two steps. First ship read-only diagnosis and blocked/alternative plans for healthy legacy, unordered revision, and interrupted operation cases. Then add scoped evidence retention and ready-to-preview requests to the matched CLI and skills. Keep the six 3.2.0 commands, their inputs, and the generation format compatible; a new skill on an older binary must report the missing capability rather than improvise. Update `kepler-lifecycle-migration` to use only the healthy route and hand semantic damage to guided recovery; `kepler-resume` remains the entry for ordinary interrupted work.

## Acceptance criteria

Use RFC 0001's synthetic cases to demonstrate that:

- The recorded stopping point yields a specific request for evidence, no writes, and no eligible hash. Supplying the evidence and exact decisions then leads to a verified continuation on the **same unit**.
- The retained-progress option preserves an applicable earlier approval, excludes an unsupported dependent, and identifies a legal next operation. A fresh reader confirms that state.
- Changed inputs invalidate approval. An interrupted exact retry finishes only its outstanding effects; a completed retry preserves subsequent work.
- A case outside the supported rules produces an escalation with the affected scope and evidence, without edits.

Better instructions alone would still make the agent assemble manifests by hand. Unrestricted journal repair would sacrifice the audit trail. The proposed interface automates the assembly and leaves the choice of lost progress and the approval of writes with the requester.
